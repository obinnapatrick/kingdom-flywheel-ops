**Result**
- **B fails** conditions 1, 2 and 8. It is killed as a Case 001 candidate.
- **A** passes or plausibly passes 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 and 9, and **partially** passes 1, 2 and 5.
  Condition 1 is the binding gap.

**Decision: NO SELECTION.**
- The arenas are **not indistinguishable**: A is clearly the stronger of the two.
- But A does not clearly pass condition 1. Primary evidence establishes that manufacturing SMEs
  *do not measure improvements well*. It does not establish that *misidentified constraints cost
  them material money*.
- It is also consistent with a rival explanation that would kill A: firm-level light-touch
  interventions do not measurably move SME performance.

**GO / NO GO on ending Phase Zero: NO GO**, by one condition, on one arena.

## Why not select A now anyway

The brief says the cost of another research round is trivial. Two peer-reviewed or institutional
randomised studies bear directly on the rival explanation, and neither has been read:

1. **Nunez-Chaim, Overman & Riom (2024), "Does subsidising business advice improve firm
   performance? Evidence from a large RCT"** (Centre for Economic Performance). This is the UK
   Growth Vouchers trial, cited by the Made Smarter evaluators as the reason naïve comparisons
   overstate impact (P02 p.3, footnote 2). It is the most relevant unread document in Phase Zero.
2. **Bloom, Eifert, Mahajan, McKenzie & Roberts (2013), "Does Management Matter? Evidence from
   India"** (Quarterly Journal of Economics). This is the randomised test of operational-practice
   interventions in manufacturing plants that underlies the ONS management-score methodology (P03
   §9 cites Bloom et al.).

I have **not** read either. Nothing below assumes what they find.

## Case 001, if the rule selects it (not selected yet)

On one production flow at one UK SME manufacturer with an order backlog: confirm the binding
constraint from two weeks of shop-floor measurement, apply one reversible zero-capex intervention
that protects constraint time, and use an A-B-A-B reversal to test whether throughput per
scheduled hour rises by at least 10% and converts into at least £500/week of contribution.

## What would most likely prove A wrong

**That, in the typical target firm, the constraint that limits profit is market demand or
pricing, not production capacity.** If so, freed constraint hours do not convert to cash, and the
capability produces measured operational gains with no economic value. Signs of this in the
first experiment: no order backlog to absorb extra output, or gains that vanish when withdrawn
(Hawthorne only).
