# C26 — Out-of-hours electricity waste in UK multi-site non-domestic buildings

Status: **SURVIVOR.** Evidence confidence: **M** that waste exists; **L** on capturable value and
willingness to pay. Rubric 72.2 (#4), robust.

## Mechanism

Shops, offices, gyms, schools and restaurants consume electricity when closed: HVAC schedules
drift, lighting and refrigeration defaults, IT left on. Half-hourly meter data reveals the
closed-hours baseload directly. Mechanism class: M7.

## Disconfirming evidence

- Diagnosis is cheap and widely offered free (supplier portals, smart-meter apps, Carbon Trust
  guides). The problem persists because fixing and *sustaining* changes needs on-site behaviour
  and controls work, not insight.
- Urgency has fallen since the 2022–23 price peak (`HYPOTHESIS`, not evidenced).
- Per-site value is modest: `ESTIMATE` a 200 MWh/yr site × 23.6p × 5–10% ≈ £2.4–4.7k/yr — close to
  the K3 threshold. Only multi-site operators clear it comfortably.

## Open gaps

1. Distribution of out-of-hours share across building types (any independent dataset).
2. Persistence of savings after 12 months (academic energy-efficiency literature).
3. Evidence that multi-site operators pay for verified savings (e.g. shared-savings contracts).

## Red team (abridged)

- **Weekend copy?** The analytics, yes. Benchmarks across thousands of sites, no.
- **Recoverable?** Yes, but persistence is the question.
- **Causation?** Good with weather/occupancy normalisation (IPMVP), but not as clean as cash
  recovery.
- **Big incumbents?** Suppliers, smart-meter platforms and BMS vendors are all positioned to give
  this away.

**Red-team verdict:** not killed. Strong measurability, weak urgency and weak capturability.
