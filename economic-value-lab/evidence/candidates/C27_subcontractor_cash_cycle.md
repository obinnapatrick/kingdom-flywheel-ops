# C27 — UK construction subcontractors under-recover what they are owed

Status: **SURVIVOR.** Evidence confidence: **M** on existence of the problem, **L** on per-firm
capturable value. Rubric 72.4 (#3), robust.

## Mechanism

Specialist subcontractors are paid through monthly applications for payment, variations and a
final account, under contracts governed by the Housing Grants, Construction and Regeneration Act
1996 (payment notices / pay-less notices). Losses arise from: under-applied work, unpriced or
disputed variations, missed statutory notice rights, final accounts never closed, and retentions
never chased. Mechanism class: M1 + M4.

- Per-subcontractor capturable value: **unknown** — the key gap.

## Red team

1. **Why unsolved?** Power asymmetry and fear of blacklisting (S27 notes subbies blacklisting
   non-payers — the reverse also happens). Tooling cannot fix power.
2. **Who solves it well?** Good in-house QSs at larger subcontractors; experienced freelance QSs.
3. **Trust?** Construction is relationship-driven and sceptical of outsiders without site
   experience. A solo non-QS operator faces a credibility gap.
4. **Build internally?** Larger subbies already employ QSs; small ones cannot afford to.
5. **Incumbents?** Main-contractor platforms (Payapps, TrakPro) sit on the other side of the
   table; they are unlikely to optimise subcontractor recovery.
6. **AI cheaper?** Helps with contract reading and notice-deadline tracking; does not remove the
   need for measured quantities and negotiation.
7. **Weekend copy?** A deadline tracker, yes. A dataset of main-contractor payment behaviour, no.
8. **Hard to copy?** Benchmarks of certified-vs-applied and days-to-pay by main contractor.
9. **Recoverable?** Partly: legacy retentions and unclosed final accounts are concrete; disputed
   variations are negotiable, not guaranteed.
10. **Large vs accessible?** The retentions pool is shrinking by law; care needed.
11. **Motivated?** Cash is existential for subcontractors — strong motivation, weak budgets.
12. **Real data?** Yes, if a subcontractor shares contracts, applications and certificates.
13. **Causation?** Good for look-back recovery (cash collected that was written off); weaker for
    forward improvements.
14. **Solo operator?** Plausible but credibility-constrained.
15. **Learning if it fails?** Contract mechanics, B2B collections, a large fragmented sector.

**Red-team verdict:** not killed. The retention component is being legislated away and the
remaining problem is partly structural. Needs independent magnitude evidence.
